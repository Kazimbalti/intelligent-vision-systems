Course Description

Intelligent Vision Systems (Applied Computer Vision) is the capstone vision course for Embedded Systems students. It develops the complete machine-vision pipeline — from photons striking a sensor to a decision produced within the memory, power, and latency budget of a constrained device.

The course begins with image formation and classical image processing, then moves through feature detection, multi-view geometry, and motion analysis, and finishes with modern deep-learning models for classification, detection, and segmentation. A recurring theme is deployment: taking an algorithm or a trained model and making it run in real time on the Raspberry Pi 5, optionally accelerated with the AI HAT/Hailo NPU or fed by a networked ESP32-CAM. The course is project-driven: each 3-hour Monday session pairs 1.5 hours of theory with 1.5 hours of supervised laboratory work, and a capstone project asks every student to build and demonstrate a real-time vision application end-to-end on the Raspberry Pi 5.

Learning Outcomes

On successful completion, a student will be able to:

  • CLO1. Explain the fundamentals of image formation, sensing, representation, colour, and processing.
  • CLO2. Apply filtering, convolution, frequency-domain analysis, edge detection, and Hough methods.
  • CLO3. Detect, describe, and match visual features and estimate geometric transforms robustly.
  • CLO4. Apply camera geometry, calibration, stereo vision, and motion estimation for 3-D and dynamic scenes.
  • CLO5. Implement classical recognition/detection (e.g. HOG, Haar, SIFT) and evaluate them.
  • CLO6. Apply machine and deep learning to image classification, object detection, tracking, and segmentation.
  • CLO7. Develop, deploy, and evaluate real-time computer-vision pipelines on embedded hardware (Raspberry Pi 5).

Course Policies

Late Work

Each student has a budget of 4 late days for the term, used in whole-day units, at most 2 per artefact. After the budget is spent, the maximum score drops 15% per day for up to 3 days, then reaches zero. Exams and the final presentation cannot use late days.

Collaboration & Academic Integrity

Discussing concepts is encouraged. The code and written answers you submit must be your own (or your team’s for the project). Cite any external code, dataset, or AI assistance you use. Copying solutions, sharing code between teams, or submitting generated text as your own analysis are integrity violations and are reported per university policy.

AI Tools

You may use AI assistants for debugging, boilerplate, and explanation. You may not use them to generate whole lab reports or exam answers. Add a one-line “AI use” note to each submission describing what you used them for.

Attendance

Lecture attendance is expected; labs are mandatory because equipment and check-offs happen in the room. Notify the instructor before a missed lab to arrange a make-up slot.

Accommodations & Well-being

Students with approved accommodations should share their letter in the first two weeks so arrangements can be made discreetly. If you are struggling, contact the instructor early — support is easier to arrange before a deadline than after.
